---
title: "He had a shape in his mind"
date: 2026-08-31
---

<p>In his sixties, my father started woodwork
    at a carpenter’s shed left behind
    by the carpenter who built our old house.
    He planed laurel planks so smooth
    that the glint of light off them
    hurt our curious eyes. He wasn’t a carpenter
    but said he had a shape in his mind.
    The only tools he had were a saw, a chisel,
    and an axe. <em>A man is no more than his tools,</em>
    he would say, if we asked him to build us a table.
    He made windows instead.
    He brought a plate and a few mugs from the kitchen,
    laid the plate on the plank, and traced half its rim
    with the pencil he kept behind his ear.
    Then, with smaller and smaller mugs,
    he drew a scallop of stacked arches.
    He then chiseled the curves.
    We had no use for windows. He kept them
    in the shed where we stored our firewood
    all through summer, and the following winter
    he built an anteroom on the second floor
    of our house and used all the windows
    there. In the evening, he’d sit smoking
    his <em>bidi</em> at one of his windows. The light
    gleamed as he drew the smoke.
    Sometimes fireflies soared from Mother’s garden
    toward that faint recognition in the window.</p>
