Last year in June, I returned to my village in the foothills of Bhutan after six
years. My father had passed away and my wife and I were returning for the funeral
services in the village, the place of my father and my childhood, and the place I
always thought was stable and secure.

After more than 20 hours, in flight and transits, we arrived in Paro, Bhutan's
only international airport, in the country's western highlands. It was summer and
balmy and, without the monsoon overcast, the sky was clean ultramarine. Although
grief was settling in, being in mountains I know and love gave me a sense of calm.
